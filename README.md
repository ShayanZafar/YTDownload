# YouTube Audio Extractor

A clean, fast web app for converting YouTube videos into high-quality MP3 audio files. Paste any YouTube link — or search for a song by name — preview it, and download the audio in one click.

## What It Does

- **Paste a YouTube URL** — Drop in any standard YouTube video link.
- **Or search for a song** — Type an artist and song title to find it on YouTube. Uploads from the artist or their label come first, marked **Official**.
- **Or paste a Spotify song link** — The app reads the song's name, artist and length from Spotify and finds the same recording on YouTube. (Spotify's own audio can't be downloaded; the audio comes from YouTube.)
- **Or open a Spotify playlist** — Paste a public playlist's (or album's) link and pick songs from it to find on YouTube and download. With a **Save to** folder, it shows which songs are already in that folder and downloads the rest for you in one go.
- **Or open a YouTube playlist** — Paste a public YouTube playlist's link (or a YouTube Music album's) to list its videos. Click one to open it, or, with a **Save to** folder, see which are already there and save the rest in one go.
- **Preview the video** — See the title and thumbnail before downloading, so you can confirm you've got the right one.
- **Extract high-quality audio** — One-click download of the audio track as a 320 kbps MP3 (the highest MP3 quality), converted from the best audio stream YouTube offers for free.
- **Browse recent extractions** — Every completed download is saved to a history list so you can quickly grab a file again later without re-pasting the URL. Cancelled or failed downloads aren't added. Search it to find a song you extracted a while ago.
- **Play your music** — A music player on the page plays the songs in your **Save to** folder on the computer running the app, and songs stored on your phone when you use the app there. Songs play untouched, at their full quality, and keep playing while you search and download.

## How To Use It

1. Start the app: on Windows, double-click `Start.cmd` in the ready-to-run app (or `scripts\windows\start.cmd` in a copy of the source code), and it opens in your browser at http://localhost:5000. [Running the app](docs/run/README.md) covers installing it on a PC, the other scripts (stop, update, start with Windows), and using it from your phone — from its home screen, too.
2. Either paste a YouTube URL into the input box (for example, `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=...`) and click **Extract**, or switch to **Search by song**, type the artist and song title, click **Search**, and pick a result. Either way, a preview card appears with the video's title and thumbnail. (The app remembers which one you used last, and it works out what you meant: a link typed into the search box is opened, and plain words typed into the link box are searched for.) You can also paste a Spotify song link (in Spotify: **Share → Copy Song Link**) into either box — the best YouTube match is picked for you, and uploads the same length as the Spotify song are marked **Same length**.
   - Copied something? Click **Paste** in the box: what you copied goes into the box and is taken as if you'd typed it and pressed Enter — a YouTube link opens, a Spotify playlist or album link or a YouTube playlist link opens the playlist panel, and anything else (a song's name, a Spotify song link) is searched for. In Chrome and Edge, once you've clicked **Allow** when the browser first asks to see the clipboard, a YouTube or Spotify link you've copied is offered under the empty box (**Link you copied**) when you open the app or come back to it: click it or press **Enter** to open it, or press **Esc** (or click its **×**) to dismiss it. A link you've opened or dismissed isn't offered again until you copy something else.
   - Working through a Spotify playlist? Switch to **Playlist**, paste the playlist's link (in Spotify: **Share → Copy link to playlist**; album links work too) and click **Open**. A panel lists its songs — type in **Filter songs** to find one — and clicking a song looks for it on YouTube, just like pasting that song's link: the best match is picked, ready to download, named "Artist - Title". The panel stays open on your next visit until you close it (**×**); the reload button fetches the playlist again if you've changed it in Spotify. The playlists you've opened are listed under the box in Playlist mode (**Recent playlists**, newest first): click one to open it again, or its **×** to take it off the list.
   - A YouTube playlist opens the same way: paste its link (on YouTube, the playlist's **Share → Copy link**, or `youtube.com/playlist?list=…` from the address bar). Clicking a video opens it, ready to download.
   - With a **Save to** folder chosen (step 5), the panel checks that folder — subfolders too, like Artist\Album — and marks each song **In folder** or not, going by the files' names and their title and artist tags. **Not in folder** shows just the missing songs. Each has a tick box, all ticked to begin with: untick any you don't want, then click **Download N songs**. The app finds and saves them one after another, with each song's progress in the list. It only downloads a song when the best YouTube match is the same length as the song on Spotify — so almost surely the same recording; any other song is marked **Check** for you to click and pick yourself. (A YouTube playlist's videos are saved as they are, named after the video.) **Stop** cancels the song being saved (nothing half-saved is left behind); pressing **Download** again carries on with what's left. Without a folder, songs you've downloaded from the panel show **Downloaded** instead.
   - Once some of the playlist's songs are in your folder, **Play N songs** plays them in the music player at the bottom of the page, in the playlist's order (**Shuffle** mixes them up; **Add to queue** adds them after the songs already lined up). **Save playlist file** saves the playlist into your folder as "Playlist name.m3u8", listing those songs in order, so VLC, Windows Media Player, foobar2000 or a phone that syncs the folder can play the same playlist. Downloaded more of its songs since? Click it again: the app updates its own playlist file (a different file with that name is never replaced; the new one is then "Playlist name (1).m3u8").
3. Not sure it's the right one? Click a search result's thumbnail to watch or listen to it right there in the list (one at a time; **Close preview**, or the thumbnail again, stops it), then **Choose this one** to pick it. Not the right video? Pick a different search result, or click **Clear and start over**. Done with the search results? **Close** them — the video you picked stays ready to download.
4. Want a different file name? Click the title in the preview card (it's underlined, with **Rename file** under it) and type a new name, like you would in a Save As dialog; Enter keeps it, Esc cancels, and **Use the original title** undoes it. Characters Windows doesn't allow in file names are swapped out (e.g. "AC/DC: Live" becomes "AC-DC - Live"); accents and other languages are kept.
5. Click **Download Audio** to save the MP3 to your device. Prefer the untouched original? Turn on **Original audio** first — the app remembers your choice, and it also applies to redownloads.
   - Want the files somewhere other than your browser's downloads folder? Just below the search box, next to **Save to**, click **Choose folder** — you can do this before opening a video. The app's folder browser opens (in your Music folder): click a folder to open it, use the shortcuts along the top (Music, Downloads, Desktop, Documents, your drives), paste a path (Explorer's **Copy as path** works), or make a **New folder**. Then click **Use “…”**. From then on, downloads go straight into that folder, with progress and a **Cancel** button on the page. The app remembers the folder, for whichever browser you use. Click the **×** next to the folder name to go back to your browser's downloads folder. A file that already exists is never overwritten — the new one is saved as "Song (1).mp3".
6. Scroll down to **Recent Extractions** to redownload any audio file you've previously fetched. The app keeps your last 1,000 downloads, 25 at a time (**Show more** for the rest). To find an older one:
   - Type in **Search your extractions**. The list narrows as you type — every word must match, in any order, and partial words count ("prog ocea" finds "Progression - Ocean Green"). Pasting a YouTube link finds past downloads of that video.
   - Matching songs are suggested below the box, each listed once (with "2×" if you downloaded it more than once). Pick one — click it, or use the arrow keys and Enter — to show exactly that song; **Show everything** undoes it.
   - Use **All**, **Past week**, **Past month** or **Older** (more than 30 days ago) to narrow by date. These combine with the search.
7. **Your music**, just above Recent Extractions, is a music player for the songs you have:
   - On the computer running the app, it lists the songs in your **Save to** folder (subfolders too), newest first — each new download shows up there. Click a song to play the list from there, or use **Play all** and **Shuffle**; **Order** sorts by title or artist instead, and **Filter songs** finds one. No folder chosen yet? **Choose the Save to folder** opens the folder browser.
   - On your phone (or any other device using the app), tap **Choose songs** and pick songs stored on that device — the songs you downloaded there, say. They play right there: nothing is uploaded. A desktop browser can **Open a folder** instead. The page can't keep them, so pick them again next time. (On the computer running the app, **Open songs** plays other files the same way, next to the folder's.)
   - The player at the bottom of the page keeps playing while you search and download: play/pause, previous (or back to the start of the song), next, a seek bar, shuffle, repeat (all songs, or this one), volume, and **×** to stop. Your keyboard's media keys and Windows' media controls work too, as do a phone's lock-screen controls.
   - Each song in the list has **Play next** and **Add to queue** buttons beside it: the song plays right after the current one, or after everything already lined up, without stopping what's playing. The player's **Up next** button shows the songs to come: click one to play it now, its **×** to take it out, or **Clear** to empty the list.

## Notes & Limitations

- The app extracts audio only — video downloads are not supported.
- **Save to** works in any browser on the computer running the app — the app saves the file itself. On another device (say, your phone using the app over Wi-Fi), downloads go to that device's downloads folder, and the app says so instead of showing the setting. Windows keeps a few folders for administrators (like the top of `C:\` or Program Files); the app tells you if you pick one — choose a folder inside your user folder instead, like Music.
- Leaving or reloading the page while a download is being saved into your folder cancels it, so the browser asks first.
- **Paste** and the copied-link suggestion read the clipboard, which browsers allow only on the computer running the app (http://localhost) or over https — not over plain http from your phone, where there's no **Paste** button (paste into the box as usual). Firefox and Safari ask every time, with a small **Paste** button of their own to click, so only Chrome and Edge offer a copied link under the box by themselves. Blocked it by mistake in Chrome or Edge? Allow **Clipboard** in the site's settings (the icon left of the address). What you copy stays in the browser unless you open it.
- Spotify: song links work in the link or search box, and playlist or album links in **Playlist** — not artists or podcasts. The `spotify.link` short links the Spotify app shares on a phone work too: the app opens them to see where they lead. No Spotify account is needed, so only public playlists can be opened (in Spotify, a playlist's **…** menu has **Make public**), and the panel lists up to 1,000 songs of a playlist. Spotify's own playlists (its mixes and editorial lists) show only their first 100 songs; for a later song, paste its own link. Liked Songs isn't a playlist Spotify shares.
- YouTube playlists: copy the playlist's own link (on the playlist's page, or its **Share** button) — a link to a video played in a playlist (`watch?v=…&list=…`) opens just that video. Only public and unlisted playlists open: not Watch later, Liked videos or the mixes YouTube makes up as you play. The app lists the first 500 videos of a playlist; private and deleted ones are left out. Its videos are saved as they are, named after the video, so the playlist panel recognises them in your folder by that name, or by their title and channel tags.
- Songs downloaded for a Spotify song are named "Artist - Title" and tagged with Spotify's title and artists and the song's Spotify id — so the playlist panel recognises them in your folder even if you rename them. For other files, it goes by their names and tags, so a file called just "Track 01.mp3" with no tags can't be recognised. It looks at up to 5,000 songs in a folder (and six folders deep).
- Every YouTube upload of a song has the same audio quality ceiling for free (about 130 kbps Opus), so for music, the artist's or label's own upload — the ones marked **Official** — is usually the cleanest source.
- The music player plays each file as it is — the browser decodes it itself, like VLC, so it sounds the same. Browsers don't play every kind of file: on the computer running the app, a song the browser can't play (WMA, APE, WavPack, AIFF in Chrome…) is converted to FLAC first — lossless, so nothing is lost — which takes a few seconds the first time (the app keeps up to 2 GB of conversions in your temp folder). On a phone, the songs never leave it, so one the phone's browser can't play is marked instead. The **Save to** folder's songs play only on the computer running the app, never on other devices on your network.
- The source video must be publicly accessible on YouTube (age-restricted, private, or members-only videos may fail).
- Downloaded files are intended for personal use. Please respect YouTube's Terms of Service and the rights of content creators.
